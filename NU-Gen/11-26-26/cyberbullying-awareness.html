Cyberbullying Awareness

Recognize harmful online behavior, respond with empathy, and know where to get help.

What cyberbullying can look like

Harassment

Repeated hurtful messages, insults, or threats.

Exclusion and rumors

Leaving someone out on purpose or spreading lies about them.

Sharing without consent

Posting private photos, chats, or embarrassing content.

Fake accounts

Pretending to be someone to embarrass or trick them.

What can I do?

Your role changes the best next step.

If it is happening to you
  • You are not at fault
  • Do not reply or retaliate
  • Save screenshots, links, and dates
  • Block and report the account
  • Tell someone you trust
If you see it
  • Do not like, share, or join in
  • Message the target privately and offer support
  • Report the content
  • Tell a trusted adult or staff member
If you caused harm
  • Stop and delete the content
  • Apologize sincerely, without excuses
  • Think about how it felt for them
  • Ask for help changing the habit

Common excuses, answered

Myth: It was just a joke.
Fact: If the other person is hurt, it is not funny.
Myth: It is only online.
Fact: The impact on mental health and school life is real.

Where to get help

For support at NU Las Piñas, reach out to Guidance Services. See the Contacts section for the office, with details to be confirmed on the official NU website. If someone is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services.
