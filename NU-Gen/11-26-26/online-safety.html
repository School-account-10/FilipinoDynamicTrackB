Online Safety

Protect your accounts, your personal information, and your digital identity with habits you can start today.

Why it matters

Your accounts hold your messages, photos, school work, and money. Protecting them protects you.

Everyday situation

A message says your school account will be closed unless you log in through a link. The page looks real, but it is a copy built to steal your password.

Everyday situation

You reuse one password everywhere. One small site is breached, and now someone can try it on your email and social media.

Everyday situation

You log in on public Wi-Fi at a cafe and forget to log out of a shared computer.

Lock it down in five steps

Do these in order. Each step makes the next one easier.

1

Use long, unique passwords

Make each password at least 12 characters, such as a few random words. Never reuse them. A password manager can remember them for you.

2

Turn on two-step verification

Even if someone gets your password, they still need the second code. Start with email, since it can reset your other accounts.

3

Keep your devices updated

Updates fix security holes. Turn on automatic updates for your phone, laptop, and apps.

4

Check before you click

Look at who sent it, where the link really goes, and whether it pressures you to act now.

5

Be careful on public networks

Avoid banking or logging in to important accounts on public Wi-Fi. Log out when you finish on shared devices.

Red flags to spot

Pressure

"Act now or lose your account." Urgency is the scammer's favorite tool.

Requests for secrets

Real offices do not ask for your password or one-time code.

Odd senders or links

Misspelled names, strange email addresses, or shortened links.

Common mistake: thinking "I have nothing worth stealing." Your account can be used to scam your friends or hide a criminal's identity.
