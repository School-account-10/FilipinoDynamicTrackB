Digital Etiquette

Respectful, ethical, and thoughtful communication in chats, comments, emails, and classes.

Be the kind of person you are offline

Online, tone is easy to misread because nobody can see your face. A little extra care goes a long way.

Do

  • Think about how your message will read to the other person
  • Give credit when you share someone's work
  • Ask before posting photos of others
  • Use clear, respectful language in school group chats
  • Disagree with ideas, not with people

Do not

  • Type in ALL CAPS to make a point
  • Post screenshots of private chats without permission
  • Spam group chats or tag people repeatedly
  • Mock someone's mistakes in comments
  • Send messages when you are angry

Say it better

Same point, better delivery.

"Wrong. Did you even read the file?""I think the deadline is Friday. Can you check the file again?"
"Why are you so slow? Reply already.""Hi, just checking in. Do you need help with your part?"
"lol look at this idiot" (with screenshot)Keep it private, or ask the person first.

Quick etiquette reminders

Emails to teachers

Use a clear subject line, a greeting, your name and section, and a polite closing.

Group chats

Mute when you need to, avoid late-night messages for non-urgent matters, and keep it on topic.

Online classes

Mute when not speaking, be on time, and respect what others share on screen.

Remember: A good rule is to write nothing you would not say to the person's face.
