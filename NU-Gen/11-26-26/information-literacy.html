Information Literacy

Think critically about what you read, check sources, and pause before you share.

Four questions before you share

If you cannot answer these, wait.

1. Who made this?

Is the source known and accountable, or an anonymous page?

2. What is the evidence?

Does it show proof, data, or a named expert?

3. Who else reports it?

Do other reliable sources say the same thing?

4. How does it make me feel?

Posts that make you angry or scared are built to be shared fast.

Kinds of misleading content

Fabricated

Made-up stories designed to look like real news.

Misleading headline

The headline says more than the article actually proves.

Old content, new claim

A real photo or video from another time or place, presented as current.

Edited or AI-made media

Images, audio, or video altered to show something that did not happen.

Satire taken seriously

Jokes and parody that spread without the context.

Missing context

A true quote or number with the important details removed.

A situation you may see

Group chat forward

A classmate sends: "Classes are cancelled tomorrow for everyone! Share this!" There is no link and no school page mentioned.

  • Check the official school announcements first
  • Ask where the message came from
  • Reply with what you verified instead of forwarding

Common mistakes

  • Reading only the headline
  • Trusting a post because many people liked it
  • Sharing first and checking later
  • Believing something because it fits what you already think
