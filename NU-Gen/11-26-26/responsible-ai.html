Responsible AI

Use AI to learn better while protecting your data and your academic integrity.

Is this a good way to use AI?

A simple traffic-light guide. Always follow each class's own policy, and ask your instructor if unsure.

Usually fine

  • Explaining a hard idea in simpler words
  • Brainstorming topics or study questions
  • Checking grammar in your own writing
  • Making a study schedule

Check the rules first

  • Summarizing readings you have not read
  • Getting feedback on a draft
  • Generating code for a project
  • Translating school material

Not okay

  • Submitting AI-written work as your own
  • Using AI during an exam when it is not allowed
  • Pasting private data or other people's details into a chatbot
  • Making fake images or voices of real people

Check what AI gives you

AI can sound confident and still be wrong.

1

Verify facts

Look up names, dates, numbers, and quotes in reliable sources.

2

Ask for sources, then open them

AI can invent references. Make sure each one really exists and says what is claimed.

3

Look for bias and gaps

Ask yourself whose view is missing from the answer.

4

Say how you used it

Follow your instructor's rules on disclosure and citation.

Never paste in

  • Passwords or one-time codes
  • ID numbers and addresses
  • Classmates' private information
  • Unpublished or confidential school documents

Remember: AI is a tool for learning, not a replacement for it. If you could not explain the work yourself, you did not learn it.
